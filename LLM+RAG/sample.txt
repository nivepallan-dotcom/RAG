 The manufacturing facility operates three primary turbine units: Turbine A, Turbine B, and Turbine C.

Turbine A experienced repeated overheating during peak production hours. 
Engineering analysis determined that the root cause was improper lubrication and delayed preventive maintenance. 
After implementing a revised lubrication schedule and automated monitoring sensors, overheating incidents decreased by 40 percent.

Turbine B exhibited abnormal vibration levels that exceeded safety thresholds. 
The issue was traced to rotor imbalance caused by uneven material wear. 
A recalibration procedure and rotor realignment reduced vibration levels by 25 percent and extended equipment lifespan.

Turbine C suffered from reduced cooling efficiency. 
Inspection revealed that airflow channels were partially clogged with debris. 
After cleaning the cooling ducts and optimizing airflow pathways, system efficiency improved by 18 percent.

A company-wide preventive maintenance strategy was later introduced. 
This included monthly lubrication checks, vibration analysis, and cooling system inspections. 
As a result, unexpected downtime across all turbine units decreased by 35 percent over six months.

Safety audits now require that all turbines operate within defined thermal and vibration thresholds. 
Continuous monitoring systems provide real-time alerts if any unit exceeds recommended operating conditions.
